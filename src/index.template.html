Train

Six sessions a week, every muscle twice. Pick a week, pick a day, tick exercises as you go. Wrecked or sleeping badly? Skip Saturday and run 5 days that week.

12-week program

Sets per muscle

Direct sets only. Presses and rows also work other muscles, and glutes also get work from squats and deadlifts. Tap a muscle to see which exercises count. The ACSM 2026 reference of about 10 sets per muscle per week comes from summaries, not the full paper.

How to add weight

Pick a weight that leaves 1–3 reps in the tankeffort
Add 1 rep to each set, every sessionreps
Every set at the top of the range? Add the smallest weight step, then restart at the bottomweight
Stuck two sessions in a row? Drop the weight about 10% and build backreset (coach practice)

Steps: about 1–2 kg for upper body, 2.5–5 kg for legs. Lower the weight over 2 seconds. Stop a set when your form breaks.

One normal Indian day

About 2,100 kcal and 140 g protein. Same on rest days. Oil is the usual error, so measure the teaspoon. Six lifting days burn a bit more than my estimate assumed, so keep 2,100 and let Progress tell you.

Where the calories come from

Protein target is 140–150 g, 1.7–1.8 g per kg. Values are my estimates from standard tables, not lab data. Weigh rice and chicken for two weeks, then eyeball.

Protein anchors

Grams of protein per portion, from my estimates. The line is 35 g, about what each main meal should reach.

2. Log food with the camera

Read this first. Photo calorie estimates are rough. Studies find wide errors, worst on big portions and mixed dishes, and I found none that tested Indian food. Hidden oil in a curry is the biggest miss. Use the camera for meals you did not cook. Your own plan meals do not need a photo.

The big apps keep the camera behind a paywall: MyFitnessPal Meal Scan and Lose It Snap It are paid in the 2026 sources I found. I could not confirm that inside the apps. These are the ones that look free or close to it.

3. Meal plans: use a free AI chat

I found no free app that makes good Indian meal plans. The paid coaching in Healthify-type apps is the main product there. A free AI chat app can do it if you give it your numbers. Check its totals, because it can add up wrong.

Prompt: a 7-day Indian meal plan
Make me a 7-day Indian home-food meal plan. About me: Daily target: 2,100 kcal, 140 to 150 g protein, about 235 g carbs, about 65 g fat. I eat: eggs, chicken, fish, dal, rice, roti, sabzi, curd, milk, soya chunks, paneer, fruit. No whey. Four meals: breakfast, lunch, a 5:15 pm pre-workout snack, and an 8:30 pm dinner. For every meal give gram weights (say raw or cooked), then kcal, protein, carbs and fat. Add up each day and keep it within 50 kcal and 5 g protein of target. Use 1 teaspoon of oil per curry or sabzi. Do not repeat the same lunch or dinner two days in a row. End with a grocery list for the week.
Prompt: estimate a meal from a photo
This is a photo of my meal. Estimate calories, protein, carbs and fat. Give a low and a high range, not one number. Tell me which part you are least sure about (oil, portion size, hidden ingredients) and ask me one question that would narrow it down. What I know: [for example: rice about 150 g cooked, 1 tsp oil in the curry, 2 rotis]

Studies of AI photo estimates: three large language models tested on food images. Giving the portion weights in the prompt is the easiest way to cut the error.

Backed by studies

  • Train each muscle about twice a week (2016 meta-analysis). Six-day Push/Pull/Legs does this. So does a 4-day Upper/Lower. When weekly sets are equal, extra training days add little (2019 review). Six days buys shorter sessions and the routine you wanted, not extra muscle by itself.
  • About 10 sets per muscle per week for growth. ACSM 2026, which I read through summaries, not the full paper.
  • Protein 1.4–2.0 g per kg (ISSN). Creatine 3–5 g a day is safe in healthy adults (ISSN).
  • Self-monitoring, motivational interviewing and sleep: tracking and sticking with a plan is linked to more weight loss (Burke 2011, weak evidence). Motivational interviewing improves adherence (Spahn 2010). Short sleep cut fat loss in a very small study (Nedeltcheva 2010). Details in What matters.
  • AI photo calorie estimates have wide error, worst on big portions and mixed dishes (study of three models). I found none tested on Indian food.
  • Measure the waist midway between rib and hip bone; keep waist under half your height (NICE). 90 cm is the South Asian screening line, not a diagnosis.

Community reports

  • The r/Fitness wiki says beginners do best on simple, compound-focused routines they can add weight to, 3–4 lifting days, some cardio kept in, and "bigger and stronger overlap".
  • A well-known Reddit beginner routine, Metallicadpa's PPL, is 6 days with steady weight increases. Jefit copies of it say so; I did not find the original thread, so treat that as a pointer, not proof.
  • This plan leans on machines. Ask your trainer to teach dumbbell and barbell basics too.

My judgement

  • The weekly check-in rules (one habit at a time, fix habits before calories, wait about 3 weeks of data) are my judgement, modelled on coaching practice, not a tested protocol.
  • Six lifting days from week 1 is your choice, not something the research asks of a beginner. The 2-sets-first ramp, the Saturday skip rule, 2,100 kcal, the step ramp, the exercise picks and the week-8 recovery week are my judgement. Adjust from your own data.
  • All food values are estimates. The 12-week results below are not promises.

Where you start

  • Belly 101.6 cm (40 in). Waist ÷ height is 0.60; NICE says under 0.5, about 85 cm for you.
  • Estimated after 12 weeks: belly 37.5–38.5 in, weight down 3–5 kg. Reaching 85 cm takes longer.
  • Calories: BMR 1,750 × 1.4 = 2,450 maintenance, minus 15% = about 2,100.
